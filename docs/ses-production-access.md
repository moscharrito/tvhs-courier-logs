# Requesting SES production access

Draft for the AWS support case that moves the account out of the sandbox.
Nothing here is submitted automatically — read it, change what is wrong, and
send it yourself.

**Where:** SES console → Account dashboard → *Request production access*. It
opens a Service Quota increase case. Region is **US East (N. Virginia)**,
which is where `SES_REGION` points and where both identities live.

**Why it is the long pole:** in the sandbox SES delivers only to verified
identities, so a courier or a pharmacist asking for a password reset is told
a code is on its way and never gets one. AWS usually answers within 24 hours
but is not obliged to. Go-live is 1 November 2026.

## Use case description

> Izy Global Services LLC operates a courier logistics platform for
> prescription delivery under contract to University Health, a public
> hospital system in San Antonio, Texas. The application is at
> https://logs.izyglobalservices.com and is used by our own couriers and by
> named pharmacy staff at nine University Health locations.
>
> All of our email is transactional and generated by an action in the
> application. We send no marketing, no newsletters and no bulk campaigns,
> and we operate no mailing lists. There are exactly three messages:
>
> 1. **Password reset codes.** A six-digit code sent only to the address
>    already recorded on that user's account, at that user's request.
> 2. **Delivery outcome notices.** When a delivery is completed or cannot be
>    completed, the pharmacy that ordered it is notified. The message carries
>    an order number, the pharmacy name, a time and a link to our portal.
> 3. **A daily performance summary** to University Health, listing counts and
>    completion rates for the previous day. Sent once per day.
>
> **How we obtain addresses.** We do not collect them. Every recipient is a
> named staff account created by an administrator of Izy Global Services
> under the terms of the contract, and the address is entered by that
> administrator. There is no subscription form and no imported list. An
> address can only receive mail from us while an active account carries it;
> an administrator removing or changing it stops all mail to the old address
> immediately.
>
> Courier applicants reach us through a public application form. They are
> not sent email by this system unless and until they become staff with an
> account, so no address obtained that way receives anything.
>
> **Bounces and complaints.** We subscribe to the SES bounce and complaint
> notifications over Amazon SNS. The receiving endpoint verifies Amazon's
> signature, the signing certificate host and the topic ARN before reading
> anything from the payload. A hard bounce or a complaint writes the address
> to a suppression list in our own database, and the sending path consults
> that list before every message and skips any suppressed address. The
> suppression is permanent until an administrator acts on it. We do not retry
> a hard-bounced address.
>
> **Unsubscribes.** These are operational messages to staff about work in
> progress, not marketing, so there is no subscription to leave. A recipient
> who no longer wants them is removed by an administrator, which stops them
> at source. Complaints received through SNS suppress the address regardless.
>
> **Volume.** University Health's own six-month delivery records average 937
> deliveries a day, 1,240 on a weekday, with a busiest recorded day of 1,875.
> Each outcome notifies the pharmacy staff entitled to see that site, which
> today is one or two people per location. We therefore expect fewer than
> 3,000 messages on a normal weekday and would not expect to exceed 5,000 on
> any day, plus one daily summary and occasional password resets.
>
> **Content.** The system handles protected health information, and none of
> it goes in email. Delivery notices carry an order number, a pharmacy name,
> a time and a link; anyone who needs to know which patient an order belongs
> to opens our portal, where access is authenticated, scoped to their own
> pharmacy and audited. This is enforced in code: the mailer refuses to send
> any message whose text matches a street address or a postal code, and the
> send fails rather than being trimmed.
>
> We send from no-reply@mail.izyglobalservices.com. The sending subdomain is
> verified in this account with Easy DKIM, and its SPF record authorises
> amazonses.com.

## Before you send it, check these

Everything above is drawn from the code and from
[docs/volume-2026-h1.md](volume-2026-h1.md).

**The three judgement calls were confirmed by the owner on 26 October 2026:**

- **Volume** — under 3,000 on a normal weekday, under 5,000 on any day.
  Comfortably inside the 50,000/day production default, so there is headroom
  if University Health add contacts per counter.
- **Who creates accounts** — Izy Global Services only. University Health's
  contract manager can reset the eight counter passwords
  (`mayResetPortalPasswords`) but does not create accounts.
- **Courier applicants** — mentioned explicitly, because a reviewer who finds
  a public form after reading "no sign-up" has a reason to doubt the rest.

Nothing in the text now needs a decision. It can be pasted as it stands.

## After it is granted

1. `/health` stops reporting `REFUSING EVERY SEND` once a real send succeeds.
2. Recipient verification stops mattering, so the reset works for any address
   on an account — which is the point of the whole exercise.
3. The 246 queued notifications from the October rehearsals will start
   sending. **Clear them first.** They are addressed to `@example.invalid`
   so they cannot reach anybody, but they will be refused and retried on
   every sweep until the rows go. See the Mail queue page under Platform.
4. The 32 accounts with no email address still cannot reset. That backfill is
   separate work and is the next thing after this.
