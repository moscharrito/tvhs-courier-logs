# Courier quick card

One page. Print it, fold it, keep it in the van.

Everything here is what the app actually says, word for word, so you can match
the card to the screen without translating.

---

## Signing in

Open the app. Tap **Staff sign in**, then your username and password.

You stay signed in for up to 30 days as long as you use it. If you have not
opened it in 12 hours it will ask again.

> **Not the driver picker.** The list of names with PINs on the first screen is
> the old TVHS system. UH couriers sign in with a username and password.

---

## Your day

You land straight on **Today**. Nothing else to find.

The top of the screen is your **next stop**. Below it, **All stops**.

Three buttons on a stop:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Directions** | Opens maps to the address |
| **Open the stop** | Where you record what happened |
| **Details** | What is in the package, who it is for |

---

## 1. Collecting at the pharmacy

Tap **Collect first** (or **Pick up** from the run).

1. **Which pharmacy** — choose where you are standing.
2. **Count the packages** — type the number you are actually holding.
3. **Pharmacy signature** — the name of the staff member handing them over,
   and their signature.

**The count is what you counted, not what the list says.** If they disagree the
app asks for a note. Write what you saw: *"One item not ready"*. Do not change
the number to make the message go away.

Collect from **every pharmacy on your run** before you set off. The app asks
you one pharmacy at a time.

---

## 2. At the door

Open the stop. Tap **I have arrived** when you get there, before you knock.

> Arriving is a time stamp, not a result. University Health measures us on
> when you arrived, so tapping it late costs us even when the delivery is
> perfect. Tap it as you park.

Then **How did it go?**

### Handed over

The normal one. Printed name of the person receiving it, then their signature.
Tap **Record the delivery**.

### Left at the door

Only appears when the medication allows it. If the screen does not show the
button, the medication needs a signature and cannot be left. The screen says
so:

> *This one needs a signature, so it cannot be left at the door. If nobody can
> sign, record it as could not deliver.*

You will be asked for a photograph.

### Could not deliver

Pick a reason **for each package**:

- Could not find the recipient
- Address is wrong
- Could not get access
- Shipment incomplete or wrong
- Recipient refused it
- Something else

Add a note if it helps the next person. This is billed as a dry run, so the
reason matters.

---

## 3. Taking things back

Anything you could not deliver comes back to a pharmacy the same day.

Tap **Take back undelivered**. The app tells you **where** to take it and
**why**; you do not have to work it out. Count the packages, get the pharmacy
signature.

---

## No signal

Keep working. Everything you tap is saved on the phone.

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **No signal. Everything you have done so far is sent.** | Nothing is waiting. Carry on. |
| **3 things are saved on this phone and not sent yet** | Normal. They will send themselves. **Try now** forces it. |
| **One thing could not be recorded** | The server said no. Read it, then **Tell dispatch what happened at these stops.** |

**Do not sign out while anything is waiting.** Signing out clears the queue, on
purpose, because the phone holds patient names and addresses. Anything unsent
is lost.

If the phone is lost or stolen, tell dispatch immediately. They can cut it off
from everything.

---

## Things that are meant to happen

- **"That order is not assigned to you."** Dispatch moved it while you were
  driving. Not your problem, not an error. Check **Today** again.
- **A delivery you already recorded shows as recorded.** The app refuses to
  record the same thing twice, including when your phone retried by itself.
- **The stop says Finished.** Somebody else recorded it, or you already did.

---

## Rules that do not bend

1. **Never record a delivery you did not make.** The signature and the time are
   evidence in a regulated chain of custody. The whole system exists to make
   the record true.
2. **Never leave a signature-required medication at a door.** The app will not
   let you, and neither will the contract.
3. **Count what you are holding.**
4. **Tap "I have arrived" when you arrive.**

Anything you are unsure about: record what actually happened, add a note, and
tell dispatch. A truthful awkward record is worth more than a tidy wrong one.
