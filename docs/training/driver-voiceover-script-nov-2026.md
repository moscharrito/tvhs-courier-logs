# Driver training — voiceover script (November 2026)

The spoken track of `driver-training-nov-2026.mp4`, slide by slide, for
anybody who would rather read it aloud and cut a human voice over the same
frames. It is generated from the deck, so it is the words that are actually
in the video rather than a draft of them.

Sixteen slides, 12m 56s. The words come from each slide's `<aside>` in the
Artifact deck "TAG Courier — Driver Training, November 2026"; edit them there
and rebuild, not here.

## 1. Cover

Welcome. This is the November 2026 version of the driver training for University Health Pharmacy Courier, and it replaces the one recorded in October. If you watched that one, several things have changed, and the next slide is the short list of them. Everything here is what the app actually does today, on the web address shown at the bottom of this slide: logs dot izyglobalservices dot com, slash u h. You do not need to install anything. It is a web page, and it works in Safari on an iPhone and Chrome on Android.

## 2. What changed since the last video

If you watched the October video, start here, because five things are different. First: there is no collection step any more. You do not tap Collect from a pharmacy, you do not count boxes into the app, and nobody at the counter signs your phone. Second: there is no separate Take a picture button. Where you need a photograph, you tap Choose File, and your own phone offers you its camera. Third: the on-shift switch has moved. It sits beside the word Today, at the top right of your round. Fourth: Take back undelivered has moved to the History tab, and Something did not match has moved to the You tab. And fifth: your stops now appear the moment dispatch assigns them to you. You are not waiting for anything before you can see your round.

## 3. Signing in

Signing in. The address is logs dot izyglobalservices dot com, slash u h. Save it to your home screen on your first day — there is no app to install and nothing to update. Sign in with the username and password dispatch gave you, and change that password the first time you are in, on the You tab. One thing to know before you need it: if you are locked out, ring dispatch. Do not rely on the Forgot your password link. It cannot send email yet, so it will tell you a code is on its way and nothing will arrive. Dispatch can set you a new password in under a minute.

## 4. Going on shift

This is the single most important habit on the whole app. Go on shift at the start of your day. The switch is beside the word Today, at the top right. A green dot and the words On shift mean dispatch can see you and can give you work. Until you tap it you are invisible to the board — no stops will come to you, no matter how many are waiting at the pharmacy. If the switch refuses to turn on, it is because your account has no pharmacy against it. Ring dispatch; it is a one-line change at their end. And at the end of the day, turn it off. If you forget, nothing breaks, but dispatch will believe you are still out driving.

## 5. Your stops for the day

Your round appears as soon as dispatch assigns it. They now choose every driver by hand, so the moment a delivery is yours it is on your phone. There is nothing to collect in the app, nothing to confirm, and nothing to wait for. Two things are on this screen. Next, at the top, is the one to drive to now — the name, the address, when it is due and how long you have. Under it, All stops is the whole round in order, five at a time, with a Next button at the bottom for the rest. Every row has its own three links: Directions opens your own maps app, Open the stop is where you record what happened, and Details is everything on record for that delivery.

## 6. Opening a stop

You are at the address. Tap Open the stop on that row, and the top of the screen gives you everything you need at the door: who it is for, where, when it is due, and the badges. Read the badges before you knock. Signature required means somebody has to sign the pharmacy's form. I D protocol means you must see identification. Refrigerated and Controlled change how the package is handled. They are not decoration — they change what you are allowed to do. There is also a Call recipient button, which dials the number on the delivery. Use it before you record a failure. Most no-answers become a successful delivery after one phone call.

## 7. I have arrived

Step one at every single door: tap I have arrived, when you get there. That timestamp is what this delivery is measured against, and it counts even if nobody answers. Tap it when you arrive, not when you finish. Two things worth saying plainly. Being late is not a problem you need to hide — an inaccurate arrival time is a much bigger one, because it is the number the hospital is shown. So never tap it from the van on the way. And if you have no signal, tap it anyway. The phone keeps it and sends it the moment you are back in range.

## 8. How did it go? Three answers

Then the app asks how it went, and there are only three answers. Handed over: somebody took it and signed the pharmacy's form. Left at the door: only when the delivery allows it — on a signature delivery that choice is not offered at all, and the screen tells you why, as you can see on the right. And Could not deliver: nobody there, refused, no access, with a reason for each item. Pick the one that is true. Could not deliver is a perfectly normal outcome and it still gets billed as a dry run, so there is no reason to avoid it. A delivery recorded as handed over when it was not is a false record on a patient's medication, and that is a different kind of problem altogether.

## 9. The photograph of the signed form

Now the photo, and this is the part that changed. There is no separate Take a picture button any more. Tap Choose File, and your own phone offers you Take Photo, Photo Library or Browse. Take Photo is the one you want. Two things go into a handover: the printed name of the person who took it, typed into the box, and a photograph of the signed form. If the delivery is marked I D protocol, there is a second photo for the identification. When you take the picture, get the whole form in frame — flat, all four corners, signature readable. That photograph is the proof of delivery. It goes into the document the hospital receives, and a blurred corner is the one thing nobody can fix afterwards.

## 10. Record the delivery, and read it back

Then tap Record the delivery, and read what comes back, because the colour means something. Green means it reached dispatch — you are done, drive on. Amber means it is saved on this phone: either you have no signal, or the photograph is still going up. It will send itself, and the important thing is that you do not record the same delivery a second time. Red means it did not save, and since the third of November that message tells you why instead of just saying try again. Read it; it usually names the thing to fix. One reassurance: a delivery you have recorded on the phone is not lost. If the phone cannot store it and you have signal, it is sent straight away instead.

## 11. Could not deliver

So what happens when you tap Could not deliver? The app asks one question per item on that stop, and nothing else — no photograph, and no name, because nobody took it. The reasons are these six. Could not find the recipient. Address is wrong. Could not get access. Shipment incomplete or wrong. Recipient refused it. And Something else, which asks you to type what happened in your own words. Choose the one that is true for each item; if a stop has three packages and two of them were refused while the third was not in the bag at all, those are two different reasons and the app wants both. You will notice that Record the attempt stays greyed out until every item has a reason. That is deliberate, not a fault — the contract charges a flat fee per item attempted, so the reason you pick is what the invoice line rests on. And remember this one, because it is the case that catches people out: if a delivery needs a signature and there is nobody there to sign, that is not a failure on your part and it is not a reason to leave it anywhere. Record it as could not deliver. The medication stays with you, and it goes back to the pharmacy the same day.

## 12. Handing back what you could not deliver

Anything you could not deliver goes back to the pharmacy it came from, the same day. Take back undelivered now lives on the History tab, directly under what you have earned — which is the screen you open at the end of a round anyway. It hands the packages back with a count and a signature from whoever receives them, and takes them off your name. Say it once and plainly: never take medication home. If the pharmacy has already shut, ring dispatch rather than leaving it in the van overnight. They will tell you where it goes.

## 13. Something did not match

When something does not match, say so. The form is called Something did not match and it is on the You tab. Use it for a wrong name, a wrong count, an address nobody lives at, a package that should have been cold and was not. You are the only person who ever sees these things. Nobody sitting at a desk can spot a label that does not match the bag in your hand, so if you do not file it, as far as the record is concerned it did not happen. While you are on that tab: it also tells you which pharmacy you drive from, with its address, and it is where you change your password.

## 14. Going off shift

Ending the day is three things. One: hand back anything you could not deliver, from the History tab, before you drive home rather than after. Two: tap Go off shift, beside the word Today, where you turned it on. Dispatch then stops offering you work. Three: if a note appears in amber saying some deliveries are still open, read it and close it — it appears once and it is telling you what is outstanding, not stopping you. That last part changed on the third of November. Going off shift is never blocked now. The app will tell you what is still open; it will not hold your shift hostage over it.

## 15. When something will not save

Sometimes something will not go through. Here is the short list. Saved on this phone: do nothing, it sends itself, and do not record it again. A red message naming a problem: read it, because since the third of November it tells you what to fix rather than just saying try again — usually a missing name or a missing photo. No room to store the delivery: free some space on the phone, or ring dispatch. The shift switch will not turn on: your account has no pharmacy against it, so ring dispatch. A stop you expected is not there: it may not be assigned to you yet, so ring dispatch. And anything you cannot explain: ring dispatch and tell them exactly what the screen said. The one thing never to do is record a delivery you did not make in order to clear a stuck screen. A wrong record is much harder to undo than a phone call.

## 16. The whole day, in six lines

That is the whole day, in six lines. One: sign in, and go on shift. Two: your stops appear — drive to the next one. Three: open the stop and tap I have arrived. Four: say how it went, with the printed name and a photograph of the signed form. Five: hand back anything undelivered, the same day. Six: go off shift. And the line to remember when none of this covers what is in front of you: ring dispatch. Never guess on a patient's medication. Thank you — and welcome to the round.
